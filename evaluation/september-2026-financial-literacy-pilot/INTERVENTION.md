# FinanceMeta Financial-Literacy Module — September 2026 v1

**Protocol:** `FINANCEMETA-LITERACY-SEP2026-v1`  
**Duration:** 35 minutes  
**Status:** frozen pre-pilot content. Any material change after the first participant starts requires a new protocol/intervention version.

## Claim boundary

This is an educational module, not financial advice. It teaches general concepts and does not recommend securities, products, lenders, brokers, or individualized financial actions. The pilot is a bounded educational evaluation and cannot establish causal effectiveness without a comparison design.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the module, a learner should be able to:

1. calculate simple annual compounding over two periods;
2. distinguish nominal growth from purchasing-power change under inflation;
3. explain why diversification reduces dependence on a single company without eliminating market risk;
4. recognize that a higher borrowing rate generally increases financing cost when other terms are equal;
5. explain why higher expected investment return generally comes with greater uncertainty or risk;
6. apply those concepts to a new budgeting or diversification scenario.

## Fixed run-of-show

### 0–5 min — Pre-assessment

Administer the frozen pre form in `protocol.json`. Collect the 0–100 confidence rating after every item. Do not reveal answers before the teaching module.

### 5–11 min — Compounding

Use only this worked example:

- Starting balance: 100 monetary units.
- Annual interest: 10%.
- After year 1: `100 × 1.10 = 110`.
- After year 2: `110 × 1.10 = 121`.

Key message: compounding applies the next period's return to the new balance, not only the original principal.

### 11–16 min — Inflation and purchasing power

Use only this worked example:

- Savings grow 3% in a year.
- Prices rise 5%.
- The account balance is larger in nominal terms, but purchasing power falls because prices rose faster.

Key message: a nominal increase does not guarantee a real increase in what money can buy.

### 16–22 min — Diversification

Contrast two hypothetical portfolios:

- Portfolio A: one company.
- Portfolio B: a broad set of companies across sectors.

Key message: diversification reduces dependence on any one company or narrow sector. It does **not** guarantee profit and does not remove market-wide risk.

### 22–27 min — Borrowing cost

Use two otherwise identical hypothetical loans with no fees and different annual percentage rates.

Key message: when amount, term, fees, and repayment structure are held constant, the higher interest rate generally produces higher financing cost. Real borrowing decisions can also depend on fees, penalties, and other terms.

### 27–30 min — Risk and expected return

Key message: offers of higher expected return generally require accepting greater uncertainty or risk. Past performance and advertised return do not make an outcome guaranteed. Learners should investigate risk rather than infer safety from return alone.

### 30–35 min — Post-assessment + transfer

Administer the frozen post form followed by the two scenario-transfer items in `protocol.json`. Collect confidence after each knowledge item. Do not coach participants during scoring.

## Instructor constraints

- Do not add a product pitch, stock pick, trading challenge, or investment recommendation.
- Do not selectively reteach an item to one participant between pre and post.
- Clarify vocabulary neutrally if necessary, but record any systematic clarification used for the cohort.
- Do not expose pre-form answers before the post-assessment is complete.
- Record operational deviations in the eventual pilot report rather than silently editing the protocol.

## Data-minimization constraints

Use pseudonymous participant IDs. Do not ask for household income, bank balances, investment holdings, brokerage usage, account numbers, or other personal financial data. Follow the host school's applicable participant/guardian consent requirements before evaluation data collection.
