## Recommended bounded ask

### A. Equity-research report template

Ask HAVEN to review one FinanceMeta equity-research memo template built around:

- investment thesis
- business model and key drivers
- industry / competitive context
- historical financials
- forecast assumptions
- valuation
- catalysts
- risks and disconfirming evidence
- sources / citations
- conclusion and decision rule

The value of HAVEN's involvement should be the quality-control layer: what a credible equity-research report must contain, what weak reasoning looks like, and what evidence standards should apply.

### B. Assessment rubric

Proposed rubric dimensions to review:

| Dimension | Proposed weight | What it tests |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Thesis and reasoning | 30% | Specificity, coherence, falsifiability, and whether the investment case is more than a narrative |
| Financial analysis and valuation | 25% | Correct use of financial statements, explicit assumptions, and defensible valuation |
| Evidence and citations | 20% | Whether material claims are sourced and the sources support the claims |
| Risk / disconfirmation | 15% | Whether the report identifies what would make the thesis wrong |
| Structure and writing | 10% | Whether the memo is clear enough to audit and compare |

**Decision required on the call:** accept, revise, or replace these dimensions. Do not describe this as a HAVEN rubric until HAVEN explicitly approves it.

### C. Seminar

**Working objective:** after September 25, participants should be able to turn raw company research into a defensible equity-research memo rather than a polished but weak stock pitch.

Suggested 45–60 minute structure:

1. What makes an equity thesis investable and falsifiable.
2. Evidence hierarchy: filings, earnings materials, industry data, and secondary sources.
3. Translating business drivers into forecast assumptions.
4. Valuation as an argument, not a spreadsheet decoration.
5. Writing risks that can actually invalidate the thesis.
6. Short teardown of a weak vs. strong memo section.
7. Participant action: revise one section of their own report using the rubric.

**Decision required on the call:** seminar date, duration, whether a participant example can be used, and whether the session may be recorded.
