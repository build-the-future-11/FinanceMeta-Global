# Standards Alignment

**Framework:** Jump$tart Coalition and Council for Economic Education, *2021 National Standards for Personal Financial Education*.

Primary source: https://www.jumpstart.org/what-we-do/support-financial-education/standards/

This lesson is designed for grades 9-12. Some concepts intentionally reinforce Grade 8 outcomes before extending to Grade 12 language.

| Lesson concept | National Standard / learning outcome | How the lesson addresses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compound interest | Saving 8-5; 8-5a, 8-5b | Learners explain compound interest and calculate annual growth when earned interest remains in the balance. |
| Compound growth transfer | Investing 8-7; 8-7a, 8-7b | Learners apply compounding to a hypothetical balance and explain why growth changes across periods. |
| Inflation and purchasing power | Saving 12-4; 12-4b | Learners explain why purchasing power can fall when nominal savings growth is below inflation. |
| Nominal vs. real change | Investing 12-4; 12-4a, 12-4b | Learners distinguish nominal growth from real purchasing-power change and optionally compute an exact real growth factor. |
| Diversification | Investing 8-5; 8-5a, 8-5b | Learners explain diversification across holdings and why a diversified portfolio can still lose money. |
| Diversification and personal constraints | Investing 12-6; 12-6a through 12-6c conceptually | The lesson introduces diversification as a risk-management concept while explicitly avoiding individualized asset-allocation advice. |
| Interest rate and APR | Managing Credit 8-2; 8-2a, 8-2b | Learners distinguish how loan costs are expressed and why APR must not be confused with the interest rate. |
| Interest rate / term and total cost | Managing Credit 8-3; 8-3a | Learners explain that higher interest rates and longer terms generally increase total borrowing cost when relevant variables are controlled. |
| Comparing borrowing terms | Managing Credit 12-1 and 12-3 | Learners are directed to compare APR and contract terms rather than infer affordability from one number alone. |
| Risk and expected return | Investing 12-3; 12-3a through 12-3d conceptually | Learners explain that riskier assets generally require higher expected returns and that potential return is not guaranteed return. |

## Important alignment boundaries

### No individualized asset allocation

Investing 12-6 includes outcomes in which students recommend allocations for different goals and risk tolerances. Five Foundations teaches only the underlying diversification idea. It does not ask a learner to choose a portfolio for themselves or recommend a security.

### APR wording

The National Standards introduce APR as a comparison measure for borrowing. The lesson adds the CFPB distinction that an interest rate is the price of borrowing principal while APR incorporates the interest rate and certain additional fees. Students are told to compare like with like and review full contract terms.

### Inflation math

The lesson does not present `nominal rate - inflation rate` as an exact real return. The optional extension uses the ratio of growth factors, which avoids turning an approximation into an identity.

## Standards consistency assessment

No lesson objective is at cross-purposes with the identified standards. The lesson deliberately narrows its scope to foundational concepts that can be taught without promoting a financial product or giving individualized financial advice.
