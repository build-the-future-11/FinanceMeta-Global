# Teacher Guide

## Before class

Print or share `STUDENT_HANDOUT.md`. Learners need a basic calculator. No account, personal financial data, or real investment information is required.

State this boundary before beginning:

> This lesson teaches general financial concepts. It is not financial advice and does not recommend any product, lender, security, or investment strategy.

## 0-3 minutes: Why the five ideas connect

Explain that saving, borrowing, and investing all involve comparisons across time. The five concepts help learners reason about how money changes over time, what borrowing costs mean, and why risk cannot be removed by a promise of high returns.

Do not ask students to disclose family income, debt, balances, investments, or account usage.

## 3-9 minutes: Compound interest

### Example

A hypothetical balance starts at 100 units and earns 10% once per year.

- End of year 1: `100 × 1.10 = 110`
- End of year 2: `110 × 1.10 = 121`

The second year's interest is calculated on the new balance, which includes the first year's earned interest.

### Check for understanding

Ask learners why the second-year increase is 11 units rather than 10.

Expected idea: the 10% rate is applied to 110, not the original 100.

## 9-15 minutes: Inflation and purchasing power

### Example

Suppose a savings balance rises 3% during a year while the general price level rises 5%.

The balance is larger in nominal terms, but its purchasing power has fallen because prices rose faster than the balance.

For an optional quantitative extension, the exact one-year real growth factor is approximately:

`1.03 / 1.05 = 0.98095`

which is about a 1.9% decline in purchasing power. Do not teach the shortcut `3% - 5% = -2%` as an exact calculation; it is only an approximation.

### Check for understanding

Ask whether “my account balance increased” necessarily means “I can buy more.” Expected answer: no.

## 15-21 minutes: Diversification

Compare two hypothetical holdings:

- Portfolio A depends on one company.
- Portfolio B spreads exposure across many companies and sectors.

Diversification reduces dependence on the result of one company or narrow sector. It does not guarantee a profit and does not eliminate losses caused by broad market declines.

### Check for understanding

Ask learners to name a risk diversification cannot remove completely. Expected examples include a broad market decline or economy-wide shock.

## 21-28 minutes: Borrowing cost, interest rate, and APR

Use two hypothetical loans with the same amount and repayment period.

Explain:

- The **interest rate** is the price charged for borrowing principal.
- The **APR** is a broader comparison measure that incorporates the interest rate and certain additional loan fees.
- When other relevant terms are held equal, a higher interest rate increases borrowing cost.
- A longer repayment term can reduce a monthly payment while increasing total interest paid.

Do not teach learners to compare a loan's APR directly with another loan's interest rate. Compare like with like and read the full terms.

### Example

Loan A and Loan B each lend the same principal for the same period and charge no fees. Loan A has the lower annual interest rate. Under those controlled assumptions, Loan A has the lower borrowing cost.

If fees differ, learners must inspect APR and the actual contract terms rather than infer total cost from the interest rate alone.

## 28-32 minutes: Risk and expected return

Explain that every investment involves uncertainty and potential loss. In general, investors demand higher expected returns for taking greater risk. A promise of unusually high returns with little or no risk should therefore be treated skeptically.

Expected return is not a guaranteed return. Riskier assets can underperform or lose money.

## 32-35 minutes: Transfer check

Learners complete the five questions in `STUDENT_HANDOUT.md` without coaching. Review with `ANSWER_KEY.md` after everyone has finished.

## Instructor safeguards

- Do not add a product pitch or stock pick.
- Do not recommend a financial provider.
- Do not collect personal financial information.
- Do not imply diversification eliminates market risk.
- Do not imply expected return is guaranteed return.
- Distinguish interest rate from APR.
- When discussing inflation, distinguish nominal from real purchasing power.
- Use hypothetical examples rather than a learner's real finances.

## Source notes

The compound-interest and inflation concepts align with Saving 8-5 and Saving 12-4 of the 2021 National Standards for Personal Financial Education. Diversification and risk-return concepts align with Investing 8-5, 8-6, 12-3, 12-4, and 12-6. Borrowing-cost concepts align with Managing Credit 8-2 and 8-3 and Grade 12 credit outcomes on APR and contract terms. See `STANDARDS_ALIGNMENT.md` for the full mapping.
